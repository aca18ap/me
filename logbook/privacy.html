Logbook

Privacy Policy

Effective date: 26-04-2026

Logbook does not collect, transmit, or sell any personal data. Everything you log stays on your device and, if you choose, in your private iCloud account. There are no analytics, no ads, and no servers of ours that ever see your data.

Data you enter

Flights, aircraft, airlines, airports, notes, and photos you add to Logbook are stored locally on your device using Apple's SwiftData framework. This data never leaves your device except through iCloud sync, described below.

iCloud sync

If you are signed into an iCloud account, Logbook uses CloudKit to sync your data across your own devices. This sync is handled entirely by Apple. We have no access to your iCloud data — it is encrypted in transit and at rest under Apple's privacy guarantees. You can disable iCloud sync for Logbook at any time in iOS Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud.

Photos

Photos you attach to flights are stored on your device in the app's private storage container. They are included in iCloud backups if you have iCloud Backup enabled. We do not access, copy, or transmit your photos.

Airline logos

Logbook displays airline logos by making a network request to a public logo service (Clearbit) using the airline's web domain as a lookup key. No personal data is sent in this request — only a domain string (e.g. lufthansa.com). Logos are cached on-device after the first fetch.

Analytics & advertising

Logbook contains no analytics SDKs, no crash reporters, no advertising networks, and no third-party tracking of any kind. We do not know how many people use the app or how they use it.

Children

Logbook is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them. Because the app collects no data at all, there is nothing specific to protect in this regard.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes materially, the updated version will be posted at this URL with a new effective date. We will not retroactively alter the terms under which data previously collected was handled — but given that no data is collected, this is a formality.
